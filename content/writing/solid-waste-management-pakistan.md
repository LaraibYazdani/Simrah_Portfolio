Solid waste management is one of Pakistan’s major environmental and urban challenges. With rapid population growth, urbanization, and industrialization, cities across the country are generating thousands of tons of waste every day. However, the systems for collection, segregation, and disposal remain inefficient and outdated.

In most urban areas, waste is either dumped in open plots, roadsides, or riverbanks, leading to pollution, foul odors, and health hazards. Lahore, Karachi, and Islamabad alone produce thousands of tons of garbage daily, but a large portion remains uncollected due to limited infrastructure, lack of awareness, and poor coordination among local authorities.

The main issues in solid waste management include inadequate waste segregation at the source, shortage of proper dumping sites, and lack of recycling initiatives. In many areas, scavengers informally sort recyclable materials, but without safety measures or proper systems.

To improve the situation, Pakistan needs modern waste collection systems, recycling plants, and public awareness campaigns promoting waste reduction and reuse. Government agencies, private companies, and citizens must work together to create a cleaner, healthier environment.
