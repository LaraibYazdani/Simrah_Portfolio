## Did you know Karachi drowns every monsoon, despite billions in “drainage projects”?

**Consider the numbers.** Karachi generates 12,000 tons of solid waste every day, yet nearly 40% goes uncollected, ending up clogging drains and stormwater channels. Billions have been poured into “nullah cleaning drives,” the Sindh government alone allocated Rs. 36 billion for drainage and sewerage in its last budget, but residents still wade through waist-deep water after every spell. This week, 235mm of rain in Manghopir brought the city to a standstill, despite official claims that drains had been cleared in advance.

> *The disaster is not rainfall, it’s governance.*

Encroachments continue to narrow key water channels, often with political protection. Sewerage pipes, many dating back to the 1960s, buckle under even light showers. Each year, billions are pledged for infrastructure upgrades, yet the city still floods after the first downpour. **So where exactly does the money go?**

Karachi contributes nearly 15% of Pakistan’s GDP and over 50% of federal revenue, yet its residents suffer Third-World urban management in the country’s financial capital. The annual cycle of “rescue pumps,” “emergency holidays,” and “damage-control press conferences” is not climate adaptation; it is institutionalized negligence.

**The truth is simple: Karachi does not lack funds.** It lacks honest governance, transparent audits, and political will. Until someone is held accountable, not just with statements but with resignations and reforms, Karachi’s monsoon story will remain the same: the skies open, the city sinks, and leaders shrug.
