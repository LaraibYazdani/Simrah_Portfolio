Art in Pakistan has a very long history. It began in the Indus Valley Civilization around 2500 BCE where people created pottery, seals, and figurines. Later, during the Mughal period, miniature painting, calligraphy, textiles, and architecture became important forms of art, supported by kings, culture, and religion.

After Pakistan’s independence in 1947, artists tried to show the identity and struggles of the new country through modern painting and sculpture. In the 1970s and 1980s, during General Zia’s rule, censorship created challenges, but artists found bold and creative ways to respond, including feminist art and new miniature painting styles.

Today, Pakistani art is recognized around the world. Many artists highlight important issues like gender equality, climate change, and urban life. At the same time, traditional forms like textiles and truck art are being preserved as part of cultural heritage.

One creative project that showed the power of art was the Truck Art Childfinder campaign. Berger Paints, Roshni Helpline, and artists painted portraits of missing children on the backs of trucks along with a helpline number. As the trucks travelled across Pakistan, thousands of people saw the portraits and some called the helpline with information. This idea helped reunite children with their families and spread awareness about child safety.

This example proves that in Pakistan, art is more than decoration. It can be a tool for social change and even save lives.
